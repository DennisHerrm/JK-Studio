Model tab: auto physics

Let physics check and correct an animation: the centre of mass, the support of the feet, feet that do not slide, a jump on a real parabola with the gravity of the game, constant spin in the air and spring chains - as a filter over your keys.

A filter over the keys#

Create the control rig, then switch on Auto physics on in the rollout Auto physics of the command panel (under IK/FK), in the menu Animation > Auto physics, in the right-click of the view (Control Rig > Auto physics) or in the search (X, F3 in Blender mode). The physics reads the pose of every frame - your FK keys and IK - and writes its own track on top; your keys stay as they are. Switch it off and the animation is exactly as before, also in the baked GLA (byte for byte). Strength mixes it in from 0 to 1. Every change of the settings is one undo step; after every change of the keys the track is computed again.

Centre of mass and support#

The view shows the centre of mass (the round symbol with two filled quarters), a dashed line down to the floor, its path over the sequence (orange in the air) and the support area of the feet on the ground (green). The masses of the body parts come from the table of D. A. Winter (Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement, after Dempster): head and neck 8.1 %, trunk 49.7 %, upper arm 2.8 %, forearm 1.6 %, hand 0.6 %, thigh 10 %, shank 4.65 %, foot 1.45 %. When the figure stands on both feet and the centre of mass leaves the support, everything turns red and the rollout names the frames - the figure would fall over. A foot touches the ground when its lowest point is at most Contact height above its lowest position in the sequence, measured above the floor (the floor of the model tab, in the mission tab the floor of the map below the foot) - models stand at different heights.

Jumps and feet#

Between take-off (the last frame with a foot on the ground) and landing the centre of mass follows the parabola of the game's gravity - g_gravity, 800 units per second squared, in the mission tab the gravity of the map. Its start and end stay where you put them; how high the figure flies follows from how long it is in the air, just like in the game. In the air the whole body also turns so that its angular momentum stays constant: swing an arm forward and the body turns back a little. Take-off and landing come from the foot contacts; Take-off here and Landing here set them at the time cursor, Automatic takes them from the contacts again. With Feet stay planted a foot on the ground does not slide: the leg IK holds its lowest point where the contact began, rolling over the ball and the height stay from your keys.

Spring chains#

Cloaks, coat tails, hair and straps can follow through: select a bone and click Chain from selected bone (also in the right-click of a control), or let Suggest chains add the hanging bones the bone recognition found. Each chain swings like a damped spring behind the bone it hangs on: Frequency sets how fast, Damping how quickly it comes to rest (1 without overshooting).

In the mission tab#

The window Animate figure has the same rollout Auto physics and a menu of the same name. There the floor is the map under the feet (traceRay) and the gravity is the one of the map (worldspawn "gravity"). The physics goes into the map GLA when you build for the game; in Movie Duels and the original JKA the figure then flies the same parabola as in the preview.
