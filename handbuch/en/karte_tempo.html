Map editor: speed and real-time light

See the light while you build: Real-time light lights the camera on the graphics card, Quick bake builds the map in the background and shows the real lightmaps. Building and editing got faster - with the same result as before.

Real-time light#

  1. 1

    Switch on Build > Real-time light. The camera (render mode textured) is lit by the lights of the map - point lights, spotlights, the sun of the sky shader (q3map_sun) and surface lights (q3map_surfacelight) - with the formulas of q3map2 -light and with shadows.

  2. 2

    Move a light or change its keys: the camera follows at once. Every light whose reach touches the picture lights it - also far away and however many there are - and every light casts shadows. Each light keeps its shadow cube until something changes within its reach; when you switch the real-time light on, all cubes are computed first (on large maps the camera shows the progress top right), so walking around computes nothing new. The cubes get the graphics memory they need, up to a quarter of the graphics card's budget (at most 2 GB): lights with a small reach get smaller cubes, and if even that is not enough, the status bar says how many lights are without shadows.

The real-time light is a preview and is never saved as a result: no bounce light, no filter, no supersampling, shadows from shadow maps instead of rays. The final light always comes from bhed_q3map2 - Quick bake or a build from the Build menu.

Real-time light like in the game#

The preview follows the rules of q3map2 -light -fast, so that the picture matches the baked lightmaps (a real JKA mapper's test map was the yardstick):

  • Sky and sun: the ray to the sun ends at the sky, as in q3map2 - the sky brush itself (caulk on its other sides, a lid above) no longer shadows the sun. sun, q3map_sun, q3map_sunExt and q3map_skyLight (many weak suns over the sky) all count; the sun has two shadow cascades (whole map, around the camera).
  • Surface light: q3map_surfacelight per triangle, divided by q3map_lightSubdivide, with the exact form factor and the back splash (q3map_backsplash); the colour from q3map_lightRGB or q3map_lightImage.
  • What casts shadows: only what casts shadows in q3map2 - world brushes and func_group (other entities only with _castShadows), no nodraw surfaces, transparent shaders only with alphashadow, patches only with Build > Patch shadows (-patchshadows) (also passed to the quick bake). Back faces go into the shadow maps, so outside light no longer leaks inside.
  • Models and patches: misc_model entities cast shadows like in q3map2 (in the world always, otherwise only with _castShadows; _castShadows 0 switches it off) and their surfaces with q3map_surfacelight shine per triangle; patches shine over the same tessellation as in -light. Brush faces shine over the triangles of -meta; q3map_sunExt with deviance and samples and lights with _samples get their jittered copies with q3map2's own random sequence.
  • Like in the game: with real-time light (and with the quick bake shown) nodraw surfaces such as caulk disappear, and transparent shaders are drawn with the alphaFunc and blendFunc of their first stage.

Light through patches: patches cast shadows only with Build > Patch shadows (-patchshadows) - as in q3map2, where they cast shadows only with -patchshadows. The switch is saved in the map (worldspawn _bhed_patchshadows) and applies to the real-time light, the quick bake and every full build: the light stage of any Build menu entry gets -patchshadows. Off, the default entries behave as in NetRadiant: only [Final] has -patchshadows, the others let the light through patches, and the build window says so. The light grid ignores patches in any case (q3map2), so models under a patch stay lit in the game.

Measured against the quick bake at fixed camera points (md_ch_ben, mp/duel1, md_eitd_jedi with sky light, a large tester map): the mean brightness difference fell from 2-95 to 0.2-2 of 255. What remains is mostly the lightmap resolution (one luxel per 16 units) and bounce light, which the preview does not have.

Built map as in the game#

Build > Built map as in the game (on by default) shows the result of the build as the game shows it: after Quick bake, or with a template .bsp, the camera draws the built map through the same renderer as the preview in the Mission tab - every shader stage with tcGen, rgbGen, tcMod and blend, the sky box, glow, fog, the game's gamma, and only what the game draws from the camera's place (PVS). The light comes from the lightmaps exactly as in the game (lightmap times 1; the editor used to double it). Selection, handles and lines stay on top as before.

Measured against the game (Movie Duels, the same maps and cameras as for the Mission tab preview): with the original .bsp as template, the floors of twelve test maps match with factor 1.00 to 1.02 (one map 1.10 to 1.20 in one corner). Map models (misc_model_static), the sky portal (misc_skyportal) and weather come through the same code as in the preview (the ship on duel_deathstar matches the game with factor 1.00); curved surfaces (patches) are divided as in the game and drop rows and columns with distance. Doors, platforms and other brush entities stand where the game puts them when the map loads (the closed blast doors on duel_deathstar match with factor 1.00), areas behind closed doors stay dark as in the game, and effects (fx_runner) and surface sprites come through the same code as in the preview. Not part of this picture: figures. The real-time light stays a preview; its tooltip says what it cannot show.

Fog, effects and sky portal#

  • Fog: brushes with a fogParms shader and the global fog of worldspawn (_fog) are drawn as the game draws them (RB_FogPass with R_FogFactor): colour and distance to opaque from the shader, the fog surface from the visible side of the brush (or q3map_fogDir), deeper below it is denser.
  • Effects: fx_runner entities play their effect with the effect system of the cutscene preview - repeat interval delay/random, direction to the target (or angle, otherwise up); START_OFF and ONESHOT wait for a use. Build > Real-time effects (fx_runner) switches them off. The editor has no .bsp, so particles do not bounce off walls.
  • Decals: impact and scorch marks of the effects land on the faces the game would mark: like q3map2, the editor builds the world of the map with its BSP tree - only the visible part of each brush face, the models of misc_model baked into the world, and only faces next to an open leaf of the tree (no mark on the floor hidden under a step). On a large map this takes a few seconds like a build; it runs in the background, and until it is done the last world counts. Faces whose shader moves its vertices (deformVertexes move) count over the whole box of the movement, as q3map2 sorts them into the tree.
  • With -meta (the default of the Build menu) q3map2 puts the faces together anew; the editor does the same and follows the BSP step of the Build menu entry you built last, also -np, -maxarea, -patchmeta and -nofog. Like q3map2 it cuts faces at fog brushes, adds the copies of q3map_cloneShader and q3map_backShader and projects _decal entities onto the world. A mark takes only the first 64 faces in its box - the same ones as in the game.
  • Sky portal: with a misc_skyportal the sky shows the scene from the portal's place, with the same view and field of view, as in the game (CG_DrawSkyBoxPortal).
  • Mirrors and portal cameras: a face with a portal shader with at least one stage (for example textures/system/mirror_yavin; a shader without any stage, such as textures/system/mirror1, is no portal in the game) and a misc_portal_surface within 64 units of its plane is a mirror; if the misc_portal_surface targets a misc_portal_camera, the face shows that camera's view (textures/system/portal fades to black beyond its alphaGen portal range). Real-time light and Built map as in the game draw them like the game and the Mission tab preview (R_MirrorViewBySurface): one view per picture, the first face in sort order that is on screen and faces the camera. Moving the misc_portal_surface or the camera, or setting the shader, changes the view in the next picture.

Quick bake#

  1. 1

    Switch on Build > Quick bake. Two seconds after the last change bhed_q3map2 builds the map in a temporary folder - -meta, -vis -fast (when the map has no leak) and -light -fast - at low priority, so the editor stays smooth.

  2. 2

    When it is done, the camera shows the real lightmaps of this build; top right it says Quick bake: real lightmaps from bhed_q3map2 ( s). Any change cancels a running build and starts a new one after the pause. Your map and a .bsp next to it are not touched.

The quick bake writes the same lightmaps, light grid and visibility as bhed_q3map2 on the command line with the same switches - the self-test compares the files. It is faster than a full build because of -fast and the missing bounce passes; for the release build use the Build menu.

What got faster#

Every change was measured before and after (median of three runs, low priority, the same maps) and gives the same result: the .bsp of -bsp and -light is byte for byte the one of a 1-thread build of Stage 6, the saved maps are identical.

  • -light uses all cores for the lightmaps (light by light in blocks, the adaptive supersampling predicted and checked exactly) and for the light grid.
  • -bsp: the T-junction fix searches in parallel and applies in order; finding equal light grid points uses buckets instead of a search through all points.
  • -vis stays as in NetRadiant-Custom: with several threads it may differ slightly from build to build, with -threads 1 it is always the same. Two ways to make it the same with several threads were tried and measured; both were slower.
  • Editor: invert selection, the selection test of the views and the NPC files of the optimizer (read in the background).
