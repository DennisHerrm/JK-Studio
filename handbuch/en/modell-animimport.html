Model tab: import animations and preset library

Bring motion from other programs onto your figure - a Mixamo FBX you downloaded yourself, glTF, BVH motion capture or COLLADA - and use the free preset library (Quaternius, 100STYLE) with one click.

Import an animation#

In the area Animation of the model tab choose Import animation (FBX, glTF, BVH, DAE) - in the group Transfer (retargeting) of the panel on the right, in the menu Animation > Retargeting, in the right-click of the view (Control Rig > Retargeting) or in the search (X, F3 in Blender mode). JK Studio reads skeleton and keys of the file, recognises known skeletons (Mixamo/HumanIK, Biped, CAT) and maps their chains, transfers the motion to your figure and plays it: a Mixamo FBX is on your figure with one click. Every animation of the file becomes a sequence of the source. Then Plot to slot writes it into the slot you choose, as with every retargeting.

Units, axes and frame rate#

Each format brings its own conventions, and JK Studio takes them from the file: FBX names its up, front and side axis and its unit (centimetres per unit) in its global settings; glTF is always metres with +Y up and the front towards +Z; COLLADA states its unit and up axis; BVH states neither, so the up axis is the one along which the body stands and left comes from the bone names. Everything is turned into the frame of the game (Z up, figure looking along -Y). The frame rate is the one of the file (FBX time mode, BVH frame time); glTF and COLLADA have none, there it follows from the spacing of the keys. Below the source the rollout shows what was read. Different sizes do not matter: the retargeting scales the steps with the leg length.

The preset library#

The group Presets starts with the library: choose a category, click an animation (a double-click shows it on your figure at once, like Preview on figure), check the Target slot (a game name such as BOTH_WALK1 is suggested) and click Put on figure (retarget). The eight categories of the design always show; one without presets yet shows a short note. In Walk and Run the list also has Walk (generated) and Run (generated) from the own generator (Speed and Arm swing under the list, the rest with Generator). Loops are cut to one seamless cycle and play in place like the walks of the game. The animations come from Quaternius (Universal Animation Library, CC0) and from "The 100STYLE Dataset - Ian Mason" (CC BY 4.0, motion capture); every entry names its source and licence, all of it is listed in data/presets/CREDITS.txt and in the About window. JK Studio ships them on their own skeletons, never with a file of the game.
